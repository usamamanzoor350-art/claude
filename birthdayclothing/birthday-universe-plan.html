2 · Diagnosis

Why sales die after 15 days

creative

One ad carries everything

Meta shows the same static image to the same people until they stop reacting. That's why a new design works for 1–2 weeks and then fades. The fix is a steady supply of new videos every week, not one perfect ad.

retention

Every sale is bought with ad money

Only 3% of customers come back. Birthdays happen every year, and every buyer has a mom, sister and friends with birthdays too. Email and SMS reminders make that repeat business almost free.

channel

One channel

94% of orders come from Meta. One bad month there, or one account problem, and the business stops. Google Shopping and Pinterest people are already searching for "60th birthday shirt".

basket

People buy one shirt

At $31 per order, the ad has to pay for itself on a single tee. Squad packs and add-ons (sash, tumbler, crown) raise the order value, so you can afford to spend more to win each customer.

trust

Late deliveries hurt the name

A birthday shirt that arrives after the birthday is worthless to the buyer. A new domain alone won't fix that; reliable delivery dates and fast support will.

3 · Brand

Birthday Universe

Position: the first place an American woman goes when a milestone birthday is coming, for herself, her mom or her best friend. Start with what already sells (milestone shirts for women 40–80), then add the rest of the day: sashes, crowns, tumblers, squad shirts, party decor.

For one day a year, everyone is the center of the universe.

For six years we've made the shirts women wear on that day: the Hello 60s, the Queen is 79s, the ones that get a hundred photos at dinner. We learned what the day needs. It has to arrive on time, it has to fit, and it has to make her feel celebrated.

Birthday Universe is everything for that day, in one place.

First draft of the brand story. It's honest about the six years, which is your strongest trust signal. We'll polish it together.

Tagline options

Three promises (only if you can keep them)

  • Here before the birthday. Show a real delivery date on every product page. Promise it only after the vendor has proven the times in test orders.
  • A real person in 24 hours. Every email and chat answered within one day.
  • Wrong size? We fix it. One free size swap.

5 · Video ads

A video engine, not a single video

The rule: make 3 to 5 new ads every week, each about 15–30 seconds, vertical, with captions, and a hook in the first 2 seconds. Keep the winners running and replace the losers every week, so there is always a fresh ad ready before the old one fades.

Reaction

"I gave my mom her 60th birthday shirt…"

A daughter films mom opening the package. The real reaction is usually your strongest ad. Get these from customers and UGC creators.

Find your year

"What year were you born? Stop scrolling when you see it."

Fast flip through 1946 → 1996 shirts. The viewer waits for their own year, so they keep watching.

Fairy tale (AI)

The princess, the witch and the tee tree.

The Omni Flash story we already wrote. Good for attention and brand feel. Test it next to real-people ads.

Squad

"When the whole birthday crew shows up matching."

Four friends at a brunch in Hello 50 shirts. Sells the squad pack.

Trust

"Ordered Monday, arrived Friday, birthday Saturday."

Unboxing with the real delivery date on screen. Answers the #1 fear directly.

Last minute

"Her birthday is in 2 weeks and you still have no gift?"

Gentle urgency with the order-by date. Works well near weekends.

Founder

"Six years, thousands of birthdays. Here's what we changed."

You or a voice-over, talking about the new promises. Builds trust for the new name.

Queen

"She's not getting older. She's getting royal."

For the Queen line: grandma in a crown with the family singing.
