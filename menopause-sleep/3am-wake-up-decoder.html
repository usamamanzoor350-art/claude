The 3 AM Wake-Up Decoder

For nights when you're exhausted but can't drift off, wake every couple of hours, or find yourself wide awake at 3 AM. Map a week of nights, see your own pattern, and build a plan that matches it.

Takes 2 minutes each morning Track for 7 nights Saves automatically on this device

You are not imagining it. Roughly 40–60% of women report sleep problems during the menopause transition, and night sweats are linked to more awakenings.1 The good news: Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I), the approach this worksheet is built on, is the recommended first treatment for chronic insomnia2,3 and has been tested specifically in women with hot flashes.4

1

Map your night

Each morning, pick a color and tap or drag across the half-hours. Don't time yourself at night. A best guess is all CBT-I asks for, and watching the clock keeps you awake.5

Sleepy, or tired but wired?

"Tired" and "sleepy" aren't the same. Sleepy means your eyes are heavy and you could nod off. Tired-but-wired means your body is drained but your brain is still switched on, which researchers call hyperarousal.6 Answer for a typical night this week.
